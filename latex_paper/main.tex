\begin{abstract}
Modern image compression necessitates a continuous trade-off between spatial quality, file size reduction, and computational complexity. While contemporary formats such as WebP and JPEG XL provide state-of-the-art rate-distortion performance, their decoding algorithms are frequently mathematically dense and computationally symmetrical. We introduce Mesh-LZ, a novel, block-based image codec designed around a dynamic spatial stencil selection process paired with Lempel-Ziv parsing and Interleaved rANS (Asymmetric Numeral Systems) entropy coding. By reducing 2D spatial pixel redundancies into 1D sequences via variance-driven heuristic stenciling (Raster, Column, and Hilbert curve paths), Mesh-LZ exploits spatial correlations with remarkable efficiency. Furthermore, for lossy applications, Mesh-LZ implements reversible YCoCg-R color decorrelation and 4:2:0 chroma subsampling, achieving highly competitive rate-distortion profiles against both JPEG and WebP. Notably, the architecture yields highly asymmetrical computational demands, resulting in decoding latencies strictly bounded to single-digit milliseconds even for dense, high-resolution imagery. This thesis details the architecture, mathematical underpinnings, algorithmic implementations, and performance benchmarking of the Mesh-LZ format across lossless, palette-reduced, and scalar quantized paradigms.
\end{abstract}
